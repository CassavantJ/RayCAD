# S3: STEP import and tessellation speed

Status: done, 2026-11-17. Spike code, raw results and this report are in `spikes/s3-step/`.

## 1. Question

How long do STEP read, transfer, healing and tessellation take in OpenCASCADE 8.0.1 on files of 5 to 100 MB,
at the three tier mesh tolerances of PLAN.md 6.2? How much memory do they need? How fast does each stage react to a
cancel request, and how long is the stage that cannot be cancelled? Is the result the same from run to run?
Which parallel strategy should the kernel use?

## 2. Short answers

1. **`ReadFile` cannot be cancelled.** It has no progress argument, the source never polls one, and
   `DESTEP_Provider::Read` called with an already-cancelled progress range still ran the whole read.
   It takes about 35 ms per MiB on this machine: 0.2 s at 5 MiB, 1.7 s at 47 MiB, 3.4 s at 95 MiB.
   Reading through `ReadStream` with our own stream buffer makes the first quarter cancellable within 5 ms.
   The remaining three quarters (2.5 s at 95 MiB) stay uncancellable.
2. **Healing is the largest import stage on the synthetic files**: `ShapeFix_Shape` took 12 s at 47 MiB and 26 to 35 s
   at 95 MiB, against 2.4 s and 4.9 s for read plus transfer. It cannot be skipped: without it, 1, 5 and 11 B-spline
   faces of F1, F4 and F5 got no triangles. One `ShapeFix_Shape` per solid on a thread pool was 4 times faster with the same result.
   On the real NIST files healing took 0.07 to 0.33 s.
3. **Tessellation with OCCT's default algorithm (Watson) is slow and memory-hungry on planar faces with many holes.**
   One plane with 825 hole wires took 2.2 s at the Low tolerance and 15.6 s at the High tolerance, and the process
   peaked at 3.1 GB for that one face. Peak working set for the 47 MiB file at the High tolerance with 32 threads was 15.6 GB,
   and 23.2 GB for the 95 MiB file.
4. **Parallel strategy.** OCCT's own `InParallel` gave the same mesh as single-threaded meshing, bit for bit, in all 83 runs,
   with no failure. One mesh job per body on a thread pool was as fast only when the file has many similar bodies, was
   1.4 to 2.0 times slower than `InParallel` on files with few or dominant bodies, and **crashed with an access violation
   in 2 of 15 runs on the 95 MiB file at the High tolerance**.
5. **The Delabella algorithm (`IMeshTools_MeshAlgoType_Delabella`) is 3 to 14 times faster and needs 5 to 20 times less memory**
   on the synthetic files. Single-threaded it gave identical results in all 36 runs. In both parallel modes it failed
   intermittently on the 95 MiB file: crashes, single faces left without triangles, and one run that returned
   no triangles at all with status 0.
6. **Run-to-run identity.** Over 3 runs (4 for F4 and F5) of every file, STEP read gave the same shape counts and the same bounding box
   to the last bit, and Watson gave the same triangle count, node count, mesh hash and mesh bounding box in every completed run and mode,
   except the one failed run listed in 6.5.
7. **The 5 s budget for "open 50 MB STEP to shaded view" is not met** on the 47 MiB synthetic file: about 21 s at the High tolerance on this
   High-tier machine, about 10 s if the first view is meshed at the Low tolerance.

**Synthetic files.** `s3gen` (source `gen.cpp`) builds mould-base-like plates with OCCT: a rounded-rectangle outline, a grid of through holes
(five diameters), counterbores and 45° countersinks on half of the holes, 3 to 6 blind pockets with filleted floors, and one pocket
per plate with a B-spline floor. Every plate has different dimensions, so no body is an instance of another. Files are written with
`STEPControl_Writer` as AP214 (`AUTOMOTIVE_DESIGN`), in millimetres, with one product per solid for the assemblies.
`make_data.sh` regenerates them; body dimensions come from the body index, so the generator is repeatable.
The files were made once and all runs used the same files.

**The synthetic files may behave differently from real customer files.** Known differences:

- About 95% of the faces are planes, cylinders, cones and tori. Real mould cavities are dominated by free-form surfaces.
- Every plate has two planar faces with 250 to 830 hole wires each. These two faces per body dominate healing and Watson tessellation time
  (section 6.3). A file without such faces will be much faster per megabyte. A file with more holes per face will be slower.
- There are no repeated instances. Real assemblies reuse parts (screws, pins), which makes the STEP file small for its triangle count.
- The geometry was written by OCCT itself. Files from other CAD systems bring tolerance and topology defects that these files do not have.
  Even so, healing was needed (section 6.2).
- No PMI, no colours, no layers.

**NIST files.** Downloaded on 2026-11-17 from `https://www.nist.gov/document/nist-pmi-step-files`
(`NIST-PMI-STEP-Files.zip`, 13,976,599 bytes, SHA-256 `8fa78429e6d8d9b0d7681d223b6aa9ec98c3772185c55b1a0e3679b21c181911`).
The NIST project page states that the test cases, CAD models and STEP files can be used without any restrictions; they are works of the
US government. The three largest files with distinct content were used. They are 3 to 6 MiB because of PMI and presentation data;
their geometry is small (125 to 487 faces). They are a real-file sanity check at the small end only.
**No real file larger than 6 MiB was measured.**

## 5. Method

`s3bench` (source `bench.cpp`) prints one line per measurement. `run_all.sh` and `run_nist.sh` ran the matrix, one process at a time.
Raw output is in `results/`. `summarize.py` builds the tables below from it, and `build_report.py` puts them into this report.

Stages, each timed with `std::chrono::steady_clock`:

1. **Read**: `STEPControl_Reader::ReadFile`.
2. **Transfer**: `TransferRoots` with the reader's shape-processing flags cleared, so that no healing runs inside it.
   Separately, `TransferRoots` with the reader default (`FixShape` on) was timed once per file.
3. **Heal**: `ShapeFix_Shape::Perform` on the whole transferred shape, after the reader was destroyed.
4. **Tessellate**: `BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh` with `IMeshTools_Parameters`: `Deflection` and `Angle` set to 0.2 mm / 20°, 0.05 mm / 12°,
   0.01 mm / 6°, `Relative` off, everything else default. Existing triangulations were removed with `BRepTools::Clean` before each mesh. Four modes:
   - **Single thread**: one mesher on the whole shape, `InParallel` off.
   - **OCCT `InParallel`**: one mesher on the whole shape, `InParallel` on (OCCT parallelises over faces).
   - **Pool**: one mesher per solid, each with `InParallel` off, on a pool of 32 `std::thread`s taking jobs from a shared counter.
     Faces outside any solid form one extra job.
   - **Pool + `InParallel`**: one mesher per solid on the pool, each with `InParallel` on.

After every mesh the program counts triangles and nodes over all faces, counts faces without a triangulation, computes the bounding box
of the mesh nodes, and an FNV-1a hash over all node coordinates and triangle indices in face order.

**Peak memory** is the process peak working set (`GetProcessMemoryInfo`, `PeakWorkingSetSize`), read after each stage. It is cumulative
within a process: the value after a stage is the highest so far, so a column can only show the cost of a stage if it exceeds everything before it.
Stages ran in the order of the table columns.

**Cancel latency.** A `Message_ProgressIndicator` subclass returns an atomic flag from `UserBreak()` and records the time of every poll.
For each stage the program first runs it uncancelled to get its duration, then repeats it three times; a watchdog thread sets the flag at a random
fraction (uniform 0.05 to 0.95, fixed seed) of that duration. Latency is the time from setting the flag to the return of the OCCT call.
"Missed" means the stage ended before the flag was set. The "longest gap between polls" of the uncancelled run is the longest time
during which OCCT did not ask; it is an estimate of the worst-case latency that three random samples can miss.
For reading there is no progress argument, so three things were measured instead: `DESTEP_Provider::Read` with a progress range that is
cancelled before the call; `ReadStream` with a stream buffer that reports end-of-file once the flag is set (5 samples); and the point in
time at which the parser has consumed the whole stream.

**Repeat runs.** F1 to F3 and N1 to N3: three complete runs. F4 and F5: one complete run, two more with pool mode only, and one more
with single-thread mode only (to save time; a complete run of F5 takes 14 minutes).

### 6.5 Parallel strategies and their reliability

Speed, Watson (section 6.3):

- **F4, 24 similar bodies:** pool and `InParallel` are equal within noise (12.3 s and 15.5 s at High; the `InParallel` value is n=1 under load).
- **F1, one body:** the pool has nothing to parallelise (33 s, same as single thread). `InParallel` gives 2.2 times, limited by the two large faces.
- **F2, F3, F5 (few or dominant bodies):** `InParallel` is 1.4 to 1.8 times faster than the pool at High.
- **Pool + `InParallel`** was never better than the better of the other two beyond noise.

Facts about the failures:

- All of them happened on F5 (95 MiB, 46 solids). None happened on F1 to F4 or on the NIST files. Whether that is the size, the number of bodies
  (more than threads), or some geometry in F5 was not determined.
- No single-threaded run ever failed, and repeated single-threaded runs in one process gave identical hashes.
- They are not memory exhaustion: the Delabella crashes happened at a peak working set of 1.7 GB with 39 GB of RAM free.
- Two crash stacks were captured (Delabella, pool mode). One is a null read in `BRepMesh_ModelHealer::closestPoints`
  (called from `connectClosestPoints`, `fixFaceBoundaries`). The other is a write to an address inside the TKMesh module from
  `NCollection_BaseList::PAppend`, called from `BRepMesh_DataStructureOfDelaun::AddLink`. Both look like corrupted or uninitialised data.
  No stack was captured for the two Watson crashes; 8 further repetitions with the stack printer did not crash.
- The Delabella pool crashes also happened when every solid was first replaced by a deep copy (`BRepBuilderAPI_Copy` with geometry), so shared topology or geometry
  between solids is not the cause, and when no progress indicator was passed, so the cancel plumbing of the benchmark is not the cause.
- The cause was not found. A defect in the benchmark's own pool code cannot be excluded, but `InParallel` with Delabella uses no benchmark threads and also failed.
- Conclusion for this OCCT build: meshing more than one shape at the same time in one process is **not safe**, and Delabella is not safe with any parallelism.

- **Read.** `STEPControl_Reader::ReadFile(const char*)` and `ReadFile(const char*, const DESTEP_Parameters&)` take no progress argument.
  In the source (`StepFile_Read.cxx`) the lexer, the parser, the record table and `StepData_StepReaderTool::LoadModel` never see a progress range.

  after 0.21 s (F1), 0.40 s (F2), 1.02 s (F3), 1.81 s (F4) and 3.87 s (F5), which is the full read time, having polled once.
  **Confirmed: `ReadFile` ignores cancel in 8.0.1.**
- **Read through `ReadStream`.** With our own stream buffer the parser stops within 1 to 5 ms when the buffer reports end-of-file (`ReadStream` then returns a failure status).
  The parser has consumed the whole stream after 26 to 28% of the read time on the synthetic files (47% on N1). Everything after that
  (building the record table and the entity model) does not touch the stream and cannot be interrupted: 0.14 s at 5 MiB, 0.28 s at 10 MiB, 0.68 s at 25 MiB,
  1.21 s at 47 MiB, 2.54 s at 95 MiB. That is about 27 ms per MiB on this machine. Uncancelled `ReadStream` took the same time as `ReadFile`.
  The stream buffer also gives byte-accurate progress for the parse phase.
- That second phase runs under a process-wide mutex in OCCT (`GetGlobalReadMutex` in `StepFile_Read.cxx`). Two STEP files cannot go through it at the same time.
- **Transfer** without healing reacts within 0.1 s (longest poll gap 0.23 s). With built-in healing: up to 0.54 s observed, longest gap 0.79 s.
- **`ShapeFix_Shape`**: up to 0.79 s observed, longest gap 1.3 s.
- **Tessellation, Watson**: up to 1.9 s at the Low tolerance, 5.4 s at Medium and **13.9 s at High**. OCCT polls between faces and at some points inside a face;
  a face that is being triangulated is finished first. The latency follows the slowest face, not the file size: F1 (5 MiB) has nearly the same worst case as F5.
  With `InParallel` or the pool, all faces in flight must finish.
- **Tessellation, Delabella** (F4 only, single thread and `InParallel`, appendix): 0.05 to 1.8 s observed; longest poll gap 0.8 to 2.2 s
  (one 7.6 s gap at High in a run that was twice as slow as its repeats, so probably machine load).
- NIST N1: every stage reacted within 90 ms.

## 7. What was not measured

- Real customer files, and any real file above 6 MiB. All sizes from 10 to 100 MiB are synthetic.
- A Low-tier or Medium-tier machine. Nothing was run on one.
- An idle machine. Other spikes ran at the same time (section 3).
- Peak memory with fewer than 32 threads, and peak memory of each stage in a process of its own.
- Cold-cache file reads. All files were in the OS file cache; disk speed played no part.
- Sewing (`ImportOptions::sew`), IGES, STEP export, XCAF import with names and colours (`STEPCAFControl_Reader`), unit conversion.
- `ShapeFix` with non-default settings, and which `ShapeFix` step costs the time.
- Assemblies with shared instances. Two instances share one `TShape`; meshing them from two threads at once would write to the same object. Not tested.
- The cause of the parallel failures (section 6.5), and whether they occur in a debug build, with TBB, or in another OCCT version.
- Cancel latency of the per-solid healing pool, and of Delabella in pool mode.
- Conversion of the triangulation into `kernel::Mesh` buffers (normals, face ranges, edge polylines) and upload to the GPU. "To shaded view" in PLAN.md 9 includes these.
- Identity of results across machines.

## 8. Recommendations

### 8.1 Budget for PLAN.md section 9, "Open 50 MB STEP to shaded view"

Measured on this High-tier machine, F4 (46.9 MiB, 24 plates, 6,294 holes), with the pipeline that showed no failures
(`ReadFile`, transfer, healing per solid, Watson with `InParallel`):

| Step | Time |
|-|-|
| `ReadFile` | 1.7 s |
| Transfer without healing | 0.7 s |
| Healing, one job per solid (n=1) | 3.0 s |
| Tessellation at the Low tolerance, `InParallel` (n=1, under load) | 4.3 s |
| **Sum, first view at the Low tolerance** | **9.7 s** |
| Tessellation at the Medium tolerance instead | 5.8 s, sum 11.2 s |
| Tessellation at the High tolerance instead | 15.5 s, sum 20.9 s |

Mesh conversion and GPU upload are not included. The current budgets of 10 s / 7 s / 5 s are not reachable with this file.

Recommended change:

1. Define the metric as **time to first shaded view, meshed at 0.2 mm / 20° on every tier**, followed by a background, cancellable refinement to the tier tolerance.
   This removes the dependence of the first view on the tier tolerance and bounds the mesh cancel latency (1.9 s worst observed at that tolerance, against 13.9 s at the High tolerance).
2. Budgets for the first view of the 50 MB corpus file:
   - High: **10 s** (measured 9.7 s on the synthetic file, with noise).
   - Medium: **15 s** [estimate: 8 cores at 0.7 of this machine's single-thread speed, speed-up 5.3 at 8 threads from the sweep].
   - Low: **40 s** [estimate: 4 cores at 0.4 of this machine's single-thread speed; read 4 s, transfer 2 s, healing 10 to 20 s, Low-tolerance mesh 13 s]. The factors 0.7 and 0.4 are guesses.
3. Add a row "refine 50 MB import to tier tolerance, background": High 20 s (measured 15.5 s, n=1), Medium 15 s [estimate], Low not applicable.
4. Add a row for **peak memory during import of the 50 MB file**. With Watson and 32 threads it was 2.7 GB (Low tolerance), 5.6 GB (Medium) and 15.6 GB (High),
   which is above the existing 4 GB memory row for the High tier. The kernel has to cap the number of faces meshed at once on large imports. The right cap needs a measurement that this spike did not make.
5. Treat all of these as provisional until a corpus of real files of 20 to 100 MB exists. The synthetic file is a hard case because of its hole-heavy planar faces;
   a real file of the same size can be several times faster or, with more holes per face, slower. The NIST files, the only real ones, are far inside any budget
   (N3, 3.2 MiB: 0.9 s in total at the High tolerance).

### 8.2 The uncancellable stage

1. Read through `ReadStream` with a kernel-owned stream buffer instead of `ReadFile`. Cancel then takes effect within 5 ms during the first 27% of the read, and
   the same buffer reports progress in bytes. It costs nothing in speed.
2. The rest of the read cannot be stopped: about 27 ms per MiB here, 1.2 s at 47 MiB and 2.5 s at 95 MiB; on a Low-tier machine about 3 s and 6 s [estimate].
   Handle it as an **abandoned job**: on cancel the job is marked cancelled and the UI is released at once; the worker finishes the OCCT call in the background and
   throws the result away. The user-visible cancel is immediate; one core and 7 to 8 MB of memory per MiB of file stay in use until the call returns.
3. A new STEP import started while an abandoned read is still running waits for it, because of OCCT's global read mutex. The job system has to expect that.
4. Show "cancelling…" honestly for the stages with long poll gaps too (healing up to 1.3 s; Watson tessellation up to 14 s at the High tolerance), or use the same abandon rule for them.
5. An option that was not measured: run import in a helper process and kill it on cancel. That gives instant cancel, returns all memory, and would also contain the crashes of section 6.5.
   Its cost is the transfer of the shape between processes, which this spike did not measure.

### 8.3 Parallel strategy

1. **Tessellation: one `BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh` at a time in the process, with `InParallel` on, Watson algorithm.** It was never slower than the per-body pool by more than noise
   on the file with many similar bodies, was 1.4 to 2.0 times faster on the others, gave bit-identical results to single-threaded meshing, and never failed in 83 runs.
2. **Do not run one mesh job per body on a thread pool** with OCCT 8.0.1. It crashed. The S3 acceptance text and kernel.h assume this strategy; both need changing.
3. **Do not use Delabella with any parallelism.** Consider it single-threaded: it is deterministic in these tests, needs far less memory, and on hole-heavy plates one thread
   of Delabella is about as fast as 32 threads of Watson. That makes it the likely best choice for Low-tier machines (4 cores, little RAM). It needs its own robustness test on real files first.
4. **Healing: one `ShapeFix_Shape` per solid** is 4 times faster and gave the same result, but was run only three times. Because concurrent meshing failed, concurrent healing needs a
   soak test (hundreds of repetitions, checking hashes) before it is adopted. Until then use the reader's built-in `FixShape`, which is faster than the explicit whole-shape call.
5. After every tessellation, check that every face has a triangulation and report the faces that do not. Do not rely on exceptions or on `GetStatusFlags()`:
   one failed run returned status 0 and no triangles.
6. Report the parallel failures to the OCCT project with the F5 file and the two stacks. A fix upstream would reopen the per-body pool and parallel Delabella.

### 8.4 Changes to PLAN.md and kernel.h

PLAN.md:

- Section 9: budget row as in 8.1; new rows for background refinement and for import peak memory.
- Section 13, S3 acceptance: "one face-mesh job per body" was measured and is rejected (crashes). Replace by "OCCT `InParallel`, one mesh call at a time".
- Section 6.2: the tolerances can stay. Note that the High tolerance costs 2.2 times the triangles and about 3 times the mesh time of Medium, and that the first view should use the Low tolerance on every tier.
- Risks: add "OCCT 8.0.1 BRepMesh is not safe when two shapes are meshed at the same time, and Delabella is not safe in parallel; found in S3".
- Risks: add "healing and Watson tessellation cost grows with the number of holes in a planar face (about 2.5 ms per hole at 0.2 mm, 15 ms per hole at 0.01 mm, 3 GB for 825 holes at 0.01 mm)". Plates with many holes are a core use case.
- The viewport budget of 500k triangles is exceeded by a 47 MiB plate assembly even at the Low tolerance (1.4 million triangles). S1 should know.

`docs/interfaces/kernel.h`:

- Header contract, "Every function is safe to call from any worker thread ... (tessellate, bvh) take an internal per-shape lock": a per-shape lock is not enough for `tessellate` and `triangulateExact`.
  They need a **process-wide mesh lock** (one BRepMesh call at a time), with OCCT `InParallel` inside.
- Header contract, "OCCT internal parallelism is OFF for anything that produces geometry (determinism)": state that tessellation is exempt. `InParallel` gave bit-identical meshes in every run here.
  For `triangulateExact` (CAM grade) the same was observed, but only on these files; keep a CI test that compares the hash of a parallel and a single-threaded mesh.
- Header contract, "Every function taking JobContext honours cancellation": add the exception for `importStep`. Proposed wording: "`importStep` cannot stop OCCT during the model-load phase of the read
  (about 30 ms per MiB of file on a High-tier machine). On cancel it returns `Cancelled` as soon as OCCT returns; callers that need an immediate reaction abandon the job."
- `ImportOptions`: add a way to choose the healing path, for example `enum class Heal { None, Reader, Full }` in place of `bool heal`, because the two healing paths differ in cost and not exactly in result.
  `None` must remain possible but is not safe as a default (faces without triangles).
- `Mesh` and `TriSoup`: add `std::vector<SubId> failedFaces` (faces that got no triangulation), or define that `tessellate` returns an error when any face fails. Today a silent partial mesh would reach the renderer and, worse, the CAM code.
- `MeshOptions`: no change needed for the tolerances. An internal choice of algorithm (Watson or Delabella) and a cap on parallel faces belong in the kernel, driven by the tier preset; they do not need to be in the public struct.
- `ImportResult`: consider a per-body flag or warning when healing changed a body, since healing changed every synthetic file.
